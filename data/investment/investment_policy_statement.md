## 1. Purpose and Scope

This Investment Policy Statement (IPS) sets out the objectives, constraints, governance arrangements and monitoring procedures that apply to the Halvorsen Ridge Balanced Mandate and, where stated, to all pooled funds managed by Halvorsen Ridge Capital Management LLC ("HRCM" or "the Firm"). HRCM is an independent asset manager headquartered in Chicago and managed approximately USD 11.6 billion in client assets as of 31 December 2025, across pooled funds and separately managed accounts.

The IPS is intended to:

1. Define the investment objectives and risk tolerance of the Balanced Mandate in clear, measurable terms.
2. Establish the strategic asset allocation and the permitted ranges around it.
3. Document the constraints (liquidity, concentration, leverage, prohibited investments) that portfolio managers must observe.
4. Assign responsibilities between the Investment Committee, portfolio managers, the Risk function and Compliance.
5. Provide a framework for evaluating performance and for taking action when results fall short.

The IPS does not replace fund prospectuses or offering documents. Where a fund prospectus imposes a stricter limit than this IPS, the stricter limit applies. Market risk limits such as Value-at-Risk (VaR) and drawdown triggers are defined in the Risk Management Framework (INV-RMF-001) and are not repeated here; this separation allows the Risk function to update quantitative limits without a full IPS revision.

## 2. Investment Objectives

### 2.1 Return Objective

The primary return objective of the Balanced Mandate is to achieve a total return, net of fees, that exceeds the US Consumer Price Index (CPI-U) by **4 percentage points per year (CPI + 4%)**, measured over rolling five-year periods.

A secondary objective is to outperform the Policy Benchmark (Section 4.3) by at least 0.75% per year, net of fees, over rolling five-year periods.

### 2.2 Risk Objective

The Balanced Mandate is designed for investors with a moderate tolerance for risk and a long investment horizon. The Investment Committee expects:

- Annualized volatility of approximately 9% to 11% over a full market cycle.
- A peak-to-trough decline not exceeding roughly 25% in a severe but plausible stress scenario.
- Calendar-year losses in no more than one year out of every five, on average.

These are expectations, not guarantees, and they are used to calibrate the strategic asset allocation rather than as hard limits.

### 2.3 Time Horizon

The Balanced Mandate assumes an investment horizon of at least seven years. Short-term underperformance is expected from time to time and is not, by itself, grounds for changing the strategy.

## 3. Governance and Responsibilities

### 3.1 Investment Committee

The Investment Committee ("IC") is the senior decision-making body for investment matters. It has **five voting members**: the Chief Investment Officer (Chair), the Head of Equities, the Head of Fixed Income, the Chief Risk Officer and one independent external member appointed for a three-year term. The **quorum is three voting members**, one of whom must be the Chair or the Chief Risk Officer.

The IC is responsible for:

- Approving and reviewing this IPS.
- Setting the strategic asset allocation and the permitted ranges.
- Approving new asset classes, new instrument types and exceptions to this IPS.
- Reviewing performance and risk reports at least quarterly.
- Placing strategies or external managers on the Watchlist (Section 9.3) and deciding on remediation.

The IC meets at least quarterly. Extraordinary meetings may be called by the Chair or by any two voting members with 48 hours' notice.

### 3.2 Portfolio Managers

Portfolio managers implement the strategy within the limits of this IPS and the relevant fund documents. They are responsible for security selection, tactical positioning within the permitted ranges, trade execution oversight and documenting their investment rationale.

### 3.3 Risk Function

The Risk function, led by the Chief Risk Officer (CRO), independently measures and reports portfolio risk, maintains the Risk Management Framework (INV-RMF-001), monitors limits daily and escalates breaches.

### 3.4 Compliance

Compliance monitors adherence to regulatory requirements, prospectus restrictions and the investment restrictions in this IPS through pre-trade and post-trade checks. Personal trading of employees is governed separately by the Compliance and Trading Restrictions Policy (INV-CMP-001).

### 3.5 Stewardship Team

Proxy voting and company engagement are delegated to the Stewardship team, which acts in accordance with the ESG and Stewardship Policy (INV-ESG-001).

## 4. Strategic Asset Allocation

### 4.1 Target Allocation and Ranges

| Asset class | Target weight | Permitted range |
|---|---|---|
| Global equities | 55% | 45% – 65% |
| Fixed income | 35% | 25% – 45% |
| Alternatives (listed real assets, liquid alternatives) | 7% | 0% – 12% |
| Cash and cash equivalents | 3% | 0% – 10% |

Within global equities, emerging markets may represent up to 20% of the equity allocation. Within fixed income, the allocation to below-investment-grade debt may not exceed 15% of the fixed income allocation.

### 4.2 Rationale

The 55/35 equity/fixed income split reflects the IC's judgment of the allocation most likely to achieve the CPI + 4% objective with acceptable volatility, based on long-run capital market assumptions reviewed annually. Alternatives are included for diversification and inflation sensitivity, while the cash allocation provides liquidity for rebalancing and client flows.

### 4.3 Policy Benchmark

The Balanced Mandate is measured against a Policy Benchmark composed of:

- 55% MSCI ACWI Net Total Return Index (USD)
- 35% Bloomberg Global Aggregate Index (USD hedged)
- 7% S&P Global Infrastructure Index (Net)
- 3% ICE BofA 3-Month US Treasury Bill Index

The Policy Benchmark is rebalanced monthly to target weights.

## 5. Rebalancing Policy

The Balanced Mandate is rebalanced back toward target weights when **any asset class drifts more than ±5 percentage points from its target weight**, or at the quarterly IC review, whichever comes first. For example, if global equities rise to 60.5% of the portfolio, the 5-point band has been exceeded and the portfolio must be rebalanced.

Rebalancing should normally be completed within **10 business days** of a band breach. Portfolio managers may use client cash flows to rebalance where this reduces transaction costs. Where market liquidity is impaired, the CIO may approve an extension of up to an additional 10 business days, which must be reported at the next IC meeting.

Tactical deviations within the permitted ranges are allowed but must be documented, along with the rationale and the expected holding period, in the portfolio management system.

## 6. Investment Constraints

### 6.1 Liquidity

At least **85% of the mandate's assets must be capable of being liquidated within 7 calendar days** without material price impact. Illiquid investments may not exceed 15% of net assets at any time. The Risk function measures liquidity daily using the Firm's liquidity bucketing model.

### 6.2 Concentration

- No single issuer may represent more than **5% of NAV**, measured at the time of purchase. Securities issued or guaranteed by the governments of the United States, Germany, Japan or the United Kingdom are exempt from this limit.
- No single industry (GICS level 2) may exceed 25% of the equity allocation.
- The ten largest positions (excluding government securities and cash) may not exceed 35% of NAV in aggregate.

If a position exceeds the 5% limit through market appreciation, the portfolio manager must notify Risk and bring the position back below the limit within 30 calendar days unless the IC approves an exception.

### 6.3 Leverage and Borrowing

The Balanced Mandate does not employ structural leverage. Temporary borrowing is permitted only to meet redemptions or settle trades and is limited to **10% of NAV**, for no more than 30 calendar days.

### 6.4 Derivatives

Derivatives may be used only for:

- Hedging existing exposures (for example, currency hedging and interest rate hedging).
- Efficient portfolio management, such as using index futures to equitize cash during rebalancing.

The **gross notional exposure of all derivatives may not exceed 20% of NAV**. Derivatives must not be used to create net exposure that would be prohibited if obtained through physical securities. All over-the-counter derivatives must be collateralized under an ISDA agreement with a Credit Support Annex.

### 6.5 Currency

Non-USD fixed income holdings are hedged 100% back to USD (within a tolerance of 95% to 105%). Equity currency exposure is left unhedged, as the IC considers equity currency risk to be a source of diversification over the mandate's time horizon.

### 6.6 Prohibited Investments

The following are prohibited unless specifically approved by the IC:

- Direct holdings of physical commodities.
- Cryptocurrencies, crypto-asset tokens and crypto-asset exchange-traded products.
- Private placements and unlisted securities.
- Short sales of physical securities.
- Securities of any issuer on the Firm's restricted list maintained by Compliance.
- Investments excluded under the ESG and Stewardship Policy (INV-ESG-001).

## 7. Selection and Retention of External Managers

HRCM may allocate up to 15% of the Balanced Mandate to external managers, primarily for alternatives. External managers are selected through a documented due diligence process that covers investment process, organization, operational controls, fees and ESG integration. The Operational Due Diligence team must issue an unqualified opinion before any allocation is made.

Each external manager receives an investment management agreement specifying its guidelines, benchmark and reporting obligations. External managers must provide holdings-level data at least monthly.

## 8. Risk Management

Risk is managed in layers:

1. **Strategic layer** — the asset allocation and ranges in Section 4.
2. **Constraint layer** — the limits in Section 6.
3. **Market risk layer** — VaR, expected shortfall, stress testing and drawdown controls defined in the Risk Management Framework (INV-RMF-001). Portfolio managers must consult the current version of INV-RMF-001 for the numerical limits in force.
4. **Operational layer** — trade controls, reconciliations and segregation of duties.

Stress tests covering at least the 2008 global financial crisis, the 2020 pandemic sell-off and a 2022-style simultaneous equity and bond decline are reviewed by the IC every quarter.

## 9. Performance Measurement and Monitoring

### 9.1 Reporting

The Risk and Performance teams produce:

- A daily risk dashboard for portfolio managers.
- A monthly performance and attribution report.
- A quarterly IC pack including performance against objectives, risk metrics, limit breaches, compliance exceptions and stewardship activity.

### 9.2 Evaluation Criteria

Performance is evaluated against the CPI + 4% objective, the Policy Benchmark and an appropriate peer group. Risk-adjusted measures (Sharpe ratio, information ratio and maximum drawdown) are considered alongside absolute returns.

### 9.3 Watchlist

A strategy or external manager is placed on the **Watchlist** if it underperforms its benchmark by **more than 3% per year (annualized) over a trailing three-year period**, or if there is a material organizational change such as the departure of a lead portfolio manager. Once on the Watchlist, the IC will review the strategy at each quarterly meeting. If underperformance persists for a further four quarters without a credible explanation, the IC will consider replacement.

## 10. ESG Integration

Environmental, social and governance factors are integrated into research and portfolio construction as described in INV-ESG-001. Firm-wide exclusions apply to the Balanced Mandate. Individual funds may adopt stricter exclusions in their own documents.

## 11. Exceptions and Breaches

### 11.1 Passive Breaches

A passive breach occurs when a limit is exceeded because of market movements, corporate actions or client flows rather than a trade. Passive breaches must be corrected within 30 calendar days unless the IC grants an exception.

### 11.2 Active Breaches

An active breach occurs when a trade causes a limit to be exceeded. Active breaches must be reported to Compliance and the CIO on the same business day and reversed as soon as practicable, normally within 3 business days. Any loss resulting from an active breach is borne by the Firm, not the client.

### 11.3 Exception Requests

Requests for exceptions must be submitted in writing to the IC Secretary, stating the limit concerned, the rationale, the proposed duration and the risk impact. Exceptions are valid for a maximum of six months and must be re-approved if still required.

## 12. Review of this Policy

The IPS is reviewed **annually by the Investment Committee at its fourth-quarter meeting**, and additionally whenever there is a material change in markets, regulation, client circumstances or the Firm's organization. Changes take effect on the date stated in the approved version.

## Appendix A — Definitions

- **NAV:** Net asset value of the portfolio or fund.
- **Drift:** The difference between an asset class's actual weight and its target weight, expressed in percentage points.
- **Illiquid investment:** An investment that cannot reasonably be sold within 7 calendar days without significantly changing its market value.
- **Policy Benchmark:** The weighted combination of indices described in Section 4.3.

For further definitions, see the Investment Glossary (INV-GLO-001).

## Appendix B — Capital Market Assumptions (2026 Review)

The strategic asset allocation is calibrated using long-run capital market assumptions (CMAs) prepared by the Quantitative Research team and approved by the IC at its fourth-quarter 2025 meeting. The CMAs are ten-year forward-looking estimates and are not forecasts of short-term returns.

| Asset class | Expected annual return (nominal, 10-year) | Expected annual volatility |
|---|---|---|
| Global equities | 7.1% | 16.0% |
| Global fixed income (USD hedged) | 4.3% | 5.5% |
| Listed real assets / infrastructure | 6.4% | 14.0% |
| Cash | 3.0% | 0.5% |
| US CPI inflation (assumption) | 2.4% | — |

On these assumptions, the strategic allocation has an expected nominal return of approximately 6.0% per year and an expected volatility of approximately 10.1% per year, before fees. After an assumed fee and cost drag of 0.45% per year, the expected real return is roughly 3.1% per year, which is below the CPI + 4% objective. The IC has accepted this shortfall for 2026 on the basis that active management is expected to contribute the remainder, and has asked Quantitative Research to present alternative allocations at the 2026 fourth-quarter review.

## Appendix C — Worked Rebalancing Example

Assume the following month-end weights for the Balanced Mandate:

| Asset class | Target | Actual | Drift (percentage points) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Global equities | 55% | 59.0% | +4.0 |
| Fixed income | 35% | 31.5% | −3.5 |
| Alternatives | 7% | 6.5% | −0.5 |
| Cash | 3% | 3.0% | 0.0 |

No asset class has drifted by more than 5 percentage points, so no band-triggered rebalancing is required. However, at the quarterly IC review, the portfolio manager may propose to rebalance toward target if transaction costs are low.

If, one month later, equities reach 60.2% and fixed income falls to 29.8%, both asset classes have breached the ±5 point band. The portfolio manager must then rebalance within 10 business days. In practice the manager would sell approximately 5.2% of NAV of equities and buy fixed income, using index futures where this reduces market impact, provided the derivatives remain within the 20% gross notional cap.

## Appendix D — Conflicts of Interest

Portfolio managers must not allocate investment opportunities in a way that favours one client or fund over another. Where an order is filled partially, the allocation is made pro rata to the original order sizes unless Compliance approves a different method in advance. Investments in funds managed by HRCM on behalf of the Balanced Mandate are permitted only where no double charging of management fees occurs; the management fee of the underlying HRCM fund is rebated in full to the mandate.

Members of the IC must declare any personal interest in a matter under discussion and abstain from voting on it. Declarations are recorded in the IC minutes.

## Appendix E — Client Communication

Clients invested in the Balanced Mandate receive a quarterly report within 20 business days of quarter end, containing performance against the CPI + 4% objective and the Policy Benchmark, the asset allocation at quarter end, a summary of significant transactions and a market commentary. Material changes to this IPS are communicated to clients at least 30 calendar days before they take effect.
