## Summary

- Global growth is slowing but not collapsing. Our base case is US real GDP growth of **1.8% in 2026**, with the euro area at 0.9%.
- We assign a **30% probability to a US recession over the next 12 months**, up from 25% last quarter.
- We expect the Federal Reserve to cut rates twice more this year, taking the federal funds target range to 3.50%–3.75% by December 2026.
- We remain modestly **overweight equities** in the Balanced Mandate (57% versus the 55% target) with a preference for quality companies, and **underweight duration** relative to benchmark.

## Macro View

Inflation has continued to moderate, with US core PCE inflation at 2.6% year on year in May. The labour market is cooling gradually: payroll growth has averaged 110,000 per month over the last three months, and unemployment has edged up to 4.4%. We read this as a classic late-cycle slowdown rather than the start of a sharp contraction.

Outside the US, China's growth remains reliant on policy support, while Europe benefits from lower energy prices but faces weak manufacturing demand. Emerging markets excluding China look more attractive than they have for several years, helped by a softer US dollar.

## Equities

Equity valuations remain elevated in the US, particularly in large technology stocks, but earnings growth has been broad enough to support current levels. In the **Global Equity Fund (HRGE)** we have trimmed semiconductors after strong gains and added to health care, where valuations are close to ten-year lows relative to the market.

On risk, it is worth reminding clients that our equity funds operate under a daily VaR budget of around 2.5% of NAV, so we have meaningful room to add risk if the opportunity set improves. HRGE's tracking error is running at roughly 4%, comfortably inside its target range.

## Fixed Income

We have extended duration in the **Short Duration Bond Fund (HRSD)** to 1.9 years from 1.6 years at the end of Q1, locking in yields that we think will look attractive once the Fed resumes cutting. Credit spreads are tight, so we are keeping high yield exposure low at about 4% of the fund, well below its 10% limit.

## Real Assets

Listed infrastructure has lagged broader equities this year as higher-for-longer rates weighed on utilities. We see this as an entry point. Grid investment needs, data centre power demand and water scarcity all support long-term cash flow growth for companies held in the **Sustainable Infrastructure Fund (HRSI)**.
