1. Purpose

This policy describes how Halvorsen Ridge Capital Management LLC ("HRCM") integrates environmental, social and governance (ESG) considerations into its investment process, the exclusions it applies, and how it exercises its stewardship responsibilities through engagement and proxy voting. It applies to all pooled funds and to separately managed accounts unless a client instructs otherwise in writing.

2. ESG Integration

Analysts assess financially material ESG factors for every company under coverage, using the HRCM materiality map, which identifies the five to eight ESG issues most likely to affect long-term value in each industry. Each company receives an internal ESG rating from 1 (leader) to 5 (laggard). Companies rated 5 may be held only if the portfolio manager documents an engagement plan, and their combined weight in any fund may not exceed 5% of NAV.

3. Firm-wide Exclusions

The following exclusions apply to all HRCM funds. Revenue thresholds are measured on the most recently reported fiscal year.

Controversial weapons (cluster munitions, anti-personnel mines, biological and chemical weapons)Any involvement (0% tolerance)

Companies in severe breach of the UN Global Compact principlesExcluded unless an active engagement shows credible progress

Individual funds may adopt stricter thresholds, which are disclosed in the relevant fund documents. Existing holdings that become subject to an exclusion must be sold within 90 calendar days.

4. Climate Commitments

HRCM has committed to reach net zero greenhouse gas emissions across its investment portfolios by 2050, with an interim target to reduce the weighted average carbon intensity of its equity and corporate bond holdings by 50% by 2030 relative to a 2019 baseline. Progress is reported annually in the HRCM Stewardship Report, published each April.

5. Engagement

The Stewardship team maintains a focus list of approximately 40 companies selected for engagement each year based on ESG rating, portfolio weight and the severity of the issue. Engagement follows an escalation path:

  1. Letter and meeting with investor relations or the company secretary.
  2. Meeting with the board chair or lead independent director.
  3. Voting against relevant directors at the annual general meeting.
  4. Co-filing or supporting a shareholder resolution.
  5. Divestment, if objectives have not been met after 18 months of engagement.

6. Proxy Voting

HRCM votes all proxies for holdings in its pooled funds, except where share blocking or excessive cost makes voting impractical. Key voting guidelines include:

  • Vote against the chair of the nominating committee (or the board chair if no such committee exists) where women make up less than 30% of the board.
  • Vote against auditor reappointment where the same audit firm has served for more than 20 consecutive years.
  • Vote against executive pay plans where more than 50% of long-term incentives vest on criteria not disclosed to shareholders.
  • Support shareholder proposals requesting disclosure of climate transition plans aligned with the Paris Agreement, unless the company already provides comparable disclosure.

Voting records are published quarterly on the HRCM website, within 30 days of quarter end.

7. Governance of this Policy

The policy is owned by the Head of Stewardship and approved by the Investment Committee. It is reviewed annually.
